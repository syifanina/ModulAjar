THE RESPIRATORY SYSTEM

1. Overview

The respiratory system is responsible for taking in oxygen (O₂) needed by the body to produce energy and releasing carbon dioxide (CO₂) as a waste product.

2. Parts and Functions

Nose

Filters, warms, and moistens incoming air using tiny hairs (cilia) and mucus.

Pharynx (Throat)

A passageway that connects the nasal cavity to the larynx, allowing air to pass through.

Larynx (Voice Box)

Contains vocal cords to produce sound and protects the lower airway during swallowing.

Trachea (Windpipe)

A main tube lined with cilia that carries air to the lungs and traps dust and germs.

Bronchus & Bronchioles

Branched tubes that direct air into each lung and divide into smaller pathways.

Lungs & Alveoli (Air Sacs)

Tiny air sacs inside the lungs where actual gas exchange takes place (O₂ enters blood, CO₂ exits).

Breathing Process

🌬️ Inhalation (Breathing In)

The diaphragm contracts and moves down, the rib cage expands, and air is pulled into the lungs.

💨 Exhalation (Breathing Out)

The diaphragm relaxes and moves up, the rib cage shrinks, and air is pushed out of the lungs.

THE DIGESTIVE SYSTEM

1. Overview

The digestive system breaks down food into nutrients that the body absorbs for energy, growth, and cell repair, and excretes the solid waste products.

2. Parts and Functions

Mouth

Teeth chew food into smaller pieces, while saliva containing enzymes starts chemical digestion.

Esophagus

A muscular tube that pushes food down to the stomach using wave-like contractions (peristalsis).

Stomach

A muscular organ that churns food and mixes it with acid and enzymes to break down proteins.

Liver

Produces bile, which helps break down fats in the small intestine.

Gallbladder

Stores bile produced by the liver until it is needed in the small intestine.

Pancreas

Produces digestive enzymes released into the small intestine to break down carbs, fats, and proteins.

Small Intestine

The main site for chemical digestion and nutrient absorption into the bloodstream.

Large Intestine

Absorbs water and minerals from unabsorbed food waste and turns it into solid stool.

Rectum

Stores solid waste (stool) temporarily until it is ready to be excreted.

Anus

The opening through which solid waste leaves the body.

THE CIRCULATORY SYSTEM

1. Overview

The circulatory system pumps and transports blood throughout the body to deliver oxygen and nutrients to cells and remove carbon dioxide and waste products.

2. Parts and Functions

❤️ Heart

A muscular organ that acts as a pump to push blood through the entire body.

Arteries

Carry oxygen-rich blood away from the heart to the body.

Veins

Carry oxygen-poor blood back to the heart.

Capillaries

Tiny, thin vessels where nutrients, oxygen, and waste are exchanged between blood and body cells.

Red Blood Cells (RBC)

Carry oxygen throughout the body.

White Blood Cells (WBC)

Fight infections and diseases.

Platelets

Help form blood clots to stop bleeding.

Plasma

The liquid part of blood that carries nutrients, hormones, and waste.

3. Parts of the Heart

Right Atrium

Receives oxygen-poor blood returning from the body.

Right Ventricle

Pumps oxygen-poor blood to the lungs to get oxygen.

Left Atrium

Receives fresh, oxygen-rich blood coming from the lungs.

Left Ventricle

Pumps oxygen-rich blood out to the body (the strongest chamber).

Valves

Flaps of tissue that keep blood flowing in one direction and prevent backflow.

THE EXCRETORY SYSTEM

1. Overview

The excretory system filters waste products, excess water, and toxins from the blood and removes them from the body as urine.

2. Parts and Functions

Aorta & Renal Artery

Carries blood rich in waste into the kidneys for filtering.

🫘 Kidney

Filters blood to remove waste (urea) and excess water, forming urine.

Renal Vein & Vena Cava

Carries clean, filtered blood back into the body's circulation.

Ureter

Tubes that carry urine from the kidneys down to the bladder.

Bladder (Urinary Bladder)

A muscular organ that stores urine temporarily until it is ready to be released.

Urethra

The tube through which urine exits the body.

⚙️ Key Mechanisms Inside Nephrons

🔍 Filtering

Nephrons filter the blood — separating waste (urea, excess salts) from useful substances.

♻️ Reabsorbing

Useful substances (glucose, needed water, minerals) are reabsorbed back into the bloodstream — nothing is wasted!

🚽 Excreting

The remaining harmful waste and excess water become urine, which flows down to the bladder and exits the body.
